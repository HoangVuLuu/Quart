1. PROJECT SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Quart is a web application that replaces the Excel sheet Philippe uses to
build the staff schedule at Presotea Montmorency, a bubble tea shop.

The problem it solves, in Philippe's words: figuring out the puzzle of who can
and cannot work on which days takes a long time every cycle. The auto-generated
schedule is the feature he asked for. Everything else in this document exists to
make that feature usable and to keep it correct after the schedule goes out.

The core loop:
  1. Philippe defines the shop's weekly shift grid once.
  2. Each cycle, staff are asked for their availability. They tap the shifts
     they can work.
  3. Philippe clicks Generate. The app produces a schedule that respects
     availability, coverage, seniority requirements and desired hours.
  4. He fixes anything he does not like by hand, checks the issues list,
     and publishes.
  5. Staff see their shifts, give away shifts they cannot work, claim open
     ones, and trade with each other, within rules Philippe controls.
  6. Philippe posts announcements, including photos of recipes, to a News tab
     only members can see.

"Quart" is Quebec French for a work shift. It is the working name in code
only; the user-facing name lives in the translation files and can change
without touching logic. Final brand name: see section 16.

      Opening  10:00-16:00  (6 h)
      Closing  16:00-23:00  (7 h)

  - 14 shifts per week, 28 slots per week, 182 scheduled hours per week.

    nothing, but workplace creation is restricted (FR-032).
  - Quebec's Law 25 applies because the app holds personal information of
    Quebec employees. See section 12.
  - The app holds business-confidential content now (recipe photos) and will
    hold highly sensitive personal information later (payslips, backlog).
    Security is designed for the payslip case from the start, so that feature
    can be added without reworking the app (section 12.7).

3.1 In scope for v1 (milestones M0-M6)
  Accounts and login, workplace membership, the weekly shift template,

  lab), manual editing, the issues dashboard, publishing with version history,

  photos), the installable app and push notifications, Google sign-in, Google
  Calendar import, and a calendar feed of your own shifts.

3.3 Backlog (agreed as valuable, deliberately not built yet)
  - Time-off requests: an employee requests a range of full days with a written
    reason explaining why. Any admin approves or declines. Approved days are
    blocked in availability forms and in the generator. Explicitly deferred.
  - Payslips: a Payslips area where the owner attaches PDF pay stubs to each
    employee's profile (drag and drop on desktop, tap to choose a file on
    phones and iPads), and a Payslips tab where each employee opens only their
    own. Not built in v1. v1 must still ship the security foundation this
    feature depends on, so it can be added later without reworking the app.
    Requirements: FR-300..311. Foundation: section 12.7.

3.4 Out of scope (will not be built)
  - Clock-in / clock-out, timesheets, worked-hours tracking
  - Payroll calculation, wages, labour cost budgets. (Delivering payslip PDFs
    produced by the owner's payroll service is a backlog item, 3.3, not
    payroll.)
  - Chat of any kind: group chat, direct messages, read receipts, message
    reactions, media in messages. Removed 2026-09-21. The News tab (6.10) is
    one-way posting by admins, not chat.
  - Multiple physical locations under one business entity
  - Any payment or billing system
  - Native iOS/Android app store applications (see NFR-012 for the PWA route)
  - Labour law compliance rules encoded in the product. Rules are configurable
    per workplace; the app does not claim to enforce any legislation.

4. GLOSSARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Workplace        A shop. Owns its own members, template, periods, settings.
  Membership       The link between a user and a workplace. Carries the role.
  Template         The repeating weekly grid of shift blocks for a workplace.
  Shift block      One entry in the template, e.g. "Monday closing 16:00-23:00".
  Shift            A dated instance of a shift block, e.g. "2026-09-14 closing".
  Slot             One person-sized opening inside a shift. A shift with a
                   headcount of 2 has 2 slots.
  Period           The stretch of dates a schedule covers. 1 to 4 weeks.
  Availability     The set of shifts one employee says they can work in a period.
  Draft            The schedule Philippe is working on. Not visible to staff.
  Published        The schedule staff see. Versioned: v1, v2, ...
  Level            Barista seniority, 1 to 3. Called "role" in early planning.
  Give-away        A published shift its holder wants someone else to take.
  Claim            A request to take an open slot or a given-away shift.
  Trade            A one-for-one swap of two published shifts between two people.
  Issue            Anything the evaluator flags about a schedule.
  Evaluator        The single component that scores a schedule and produces
                   the issue list. Shared by the generator and the dashboard.
  Availability     An optional note an employee attaches to their availability
  comment          for a period. Admins only (FR-103).
  Announcement     A post by an admin in the News tab: text, photos, or both.
  Stored file      Any uploaded file. Has a scope that decides who may read it
                   (AD-017). Announcement photos now, payslips later.
  Permission       A named right such as ManageSchedule or ManagePayslips.
                   Roles are bundles of permissions (5.5).
  Step-up          Asking a signed-in user to confirm their password again
                   before a sensitive action (AD-044).
  Payslip          (Backlog) A PDF pay stub produced by the owner's payroll
                   service and attached to one employee.

5. ROLES AND PERMISSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Principle (AD-001)
Roles do not belong to accounts. They belong to memberships. One person can be
an admin of one workplace and an employee of another. The sign-up form does not
ask whether you are a manager.

5.2 Roles
  Platform admin   A flag on the user account, not a membership. Hoang only.
                   Can create workplaces and access support tooling. Is not
                   automatically a member of any workplace, is never scheduled,
                   and can never read availability comments or, later,
                   payslips through the app (see 12.5 on infrastructure access).
  Owner            One per workplace. Everything an admin can do, plus transfer
                   ownership and delete the workplace.
  Admin            Full management of the workplace. There may be several.
  Employee         Member of the workplace, scheduled, no management rights.

5.3 Permission matrix
  Action                                   Owner  Admin  Employee
  Edit workplace info and settings           Y      Y       N
  Edit the shift template                    Y      Y       N
  Assign barista levels to members           Y      Y       N
  Regenerate / rotate the join code          Y      Y       N
  Remove a member                            Y      Y       N
  Promote or demote an admin                 Y      N       N
  Transfer ownership                         Y      N       N
  Delete the workplace                       Y      N       N
  Open / close an availability cycle         Y      Y       N
  Reopen one person's availability           Y      Y       N
  Generate, edit, publish a schedule         Y      Y       N
  Approve claims and trades                  Y      Y       N
  Post announcements, with photos            Y      Y       N
  Edit or delete any announcement            Y      Y       N
  React to announcements                     Y      Y       Y
  Read others' availability comments         Y      Y       N
  Submit availability                        Y*     Y*      Y
  Be scheduled on shifts                     Y*     Y*      Y
  Give away / claim / trade shifts           Y*     Y*      Y
  Manage payslips (backlog)                  Y      P       N
  Open own payslips (backlog)                Y*     Y*      Y
  * only when "I work shifts too" is enabled on that membership (FR-036)
  P only if the owner explicitly grants ManagePayslips to that admin. Being an
    admin is not enough to see anyone's pay.

5.4 Levels are admin-assigned (FR-035)
Barista level is a property of the membership, set only by an owner or admin.
Employees can see their own level and their coworkers' levels but can never
change any level, including their own. Changing a level takes effect
immediately for the generator and the evaluator; it does not retroactively
alter an already published schedule.

5.5 Permissions, not role checks (AD-042)
Code never asks "is this person an admin?". It asks for a named permission:
  ManageWorkplace, ManageMembers, ManageLevels, ManageTemplate,
  ManageSchedule, ApproveRequests, PostAnnouncements,
  ViewAvailabilityComments, TransferOwnership, DeleteWorkplace,
  ManagePayslips (backlog).
Owner holds all of them. Admin holds all except TransferOwnership,
DeleteWorkplace and ManagePayslips. Employee holds none. Individual
permissions can be granted to a specific membership on top of its role; that
is how an owner would let a trusted admin handle payslips. This is the piece
that lets payslips arrive later as one new permission instead of a new set of
rules scattered through the code.

6. FUNCTIONAL SPECIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 ACCOUNTS AND AUTHENTICATION
FR-001  Sign up with first name, last name, email, password, confirm password.
        All fields mandatory. No phone number is collected anywhere in the app.
FR-002  Names accept accents, hyphens and apostrophes (Marc-Olivier, Côté,
        O'Connor). Do not restrict to ASCII letters.
FR-003  Password minimum length 8 characters. No forced symbol/digit/case
        composition rules. Check the password against a breached-password list
        using the Have I Been Pwned range API (k-anonymity: only the first 5
        characters of the SHA-1 hash leave the server; the password never does).
        Reject known-breached passwords with a clear message.
FR-004  Email verification is required before the account can be used.
        Verification link is single use and expires in 24 hours.
FR-005  Forgot password sends an email containing a link to a reset page on the
        site with new password and confirm password fields. The token expires in
        30-60 minutes, works exactly once, and successful reset signs out all
        other sessions.
FR-006  Sign in with Google is offered alongside email and password. Google
        OAuth is needed anyway for Calendar import, so the login integration is
        nearly free. Matching is by verified email address.
FR-007  Login is rate limited and the account locks temporarily after repeated
        failures. Rate limits also apply to password reset requests and to join
        code attempts.
FR-008  A user can change their display name, language and notification
        preferences. Changing email requires re-verification.
FR-009  A user can delete their account. On deletion: profile and their
        availability comments are deleted; their name in past schedules is
        replaced by "Former employee" so historical schedules stay readable;
        announcements they posted stay, attributed to "Former member".

6.2 WORKPLACES AND MEMBERSHIP
FR-030  A workplace has a name and an address, both entered at creation.
FR-031  A workplace has a time zone. Default America/Toronto (covers Montreal).
FR-032  Only a platform admin can create a workplace. Anyone can create an
        account, but an account with no membership can do nothing except join
        with a code. This prevents strangers creating workplaces on free-tier
        infrastructure. The data model itself supports many workplaces.
FR-033  Every workplace has a join code: 10 characters, uppercase letters and
        digits, excluding look-alike characters (no 0/O, no 1/I/L). Matching is
        case-insensitive. An admin can regenerate the code at any time, which
        invalidates the old one. The code can also be shared as a link or QR
        code that pre-fills the join screen.
FR-034  Joining is instant by default. An optional workplace setting requires
        admin approval before a new member becomes active.
FR-035  An admin assigns each member a barista level (1, 2 or 3). See 5.4.
FR-036  Each membership has an "I work shifts too" flag. When on, that member
        submits availability, has a weekly desired-hours target, and can be
        scheduled. Philippe has this on.
FR-037  Each member who works shifts has a weekly desired hours target, and an
        optional maximum set by an admin. There is no minimum-hours concept.
FR-038  A member can leave a workplace. An admin can remove a member. In both
        cases every future shift held by that person becomes an open slot and
        the admins are notified. Past shifts are left intact.
FR-039  One user can be a member of several workplaces at once.
FR-040  Members can see each other's name, level, contact email, and the full
        team schedule. No other personal information is exposed. Availability
        comments are never shown to coworkers.
FR-041  Ownership can be transferred to another admin of the same workplace.
        The transfer requires confirmation from the current owner.
FR-042  Creating a workplace produces a single-use owner invitation link, valid
        7 days, which the platform admin sends to the future owner. Whoever
        signs in and accepts it becomes the owner. The platform admin never
        becomes a member, and can revoke and reissue the link while it is
        unused. Until it is accepted the workplace has no members.

6.3 SHIFT TEMPLATE
FR-060  The template is a weekly grid, Monday to Sunday. Each shift block has a
        day, a start time, an end time, a headcount and its level requirements.
FR-061  Times snap to 15 minutes.
FR-062  No shift may cross midnight. End time must be after start time on the
        same day. Reject anything else at validation time.
FR-063  Shift blocks on the same day may overlap (e.g. an opener and a mid).
        The shop does not use this today but the model must allow it.
FR-064  Headcount is an exact target number per shift block, adjustable with
        a plus/minus control. A shift below its headcount is reported as an
        issue to the admin and appears to employees as an open shift.
FR-065  Each shift block can require a minimum quantity of a given level, for
        example "at least 1 level 3". Requirements are configured per shift
        block, not globally, and count inside the headcount rather than on top
        of it (3 people of whom at least 1 is level 3, not 3 plus 1).
FR-066  Because levels are a ladder, a requirement for level N is satisfied by
        anyone at level N or above.
FR-067  Desktop and iPad support drag-to-create on a week grid, the way Google
        Calendar creates events. Phone and iPad also support a tap-only path:
        tap an empty slot, enter start and end time, choose which days it
        repeats on. The tap path must be able to do everything the drag path
        can do. Presotea's whole template is created by entering 10:00-16:00
        once and applying it to all seven days, then 16:00-23:00 likewise.
FR-068  Editing the template affects future, unpublished periods only. It never
        rewrites a published schedule.
FR-069  If a shift block is changed after availability has been collected for a
        period that uses it, every answer for that block resets to unanswered
        and the affected employees are notified.

6.4 AVAILABILITY CYCLES
FR-090  The admin sets a period length of 1, 2, 3 or 4 weeks. "Monthly" means
        4-week blocks, not calendar months, so that weeks and hours targets
        stay aligned.
FR-091  The admin sets how many days before the period starts the availability
        request goes out, and a submission deadline. Automatic reminders are
        sent to anyone who has not submitted.
FR-092  Starting a period copies the template into real, dated shifts. From that
        point the period has its own shifts, which the admin may edit for that
        period only (a holiday closure, extended December hours).
FR-093  An employee sets availability by tapping the shift blocks they can work,
        for each date in the period. There is no free-form time painting: the
        unit of availability is the shift the shop actually runs.
FR-094  Availability is per workplace, since it is expressed in that workplace's
        shifts. Shifts the employee already holds at another workplace grey out
        the overlapping blocks automatically. The other workplace's admin sees
        only "unavailable" and never where the person is working.
FR-095  Helper: "Same as last period" loads the previous period's answers so
        they can be extended or edited before sending.
FR-096  Helper: "Copy week 1 to the rest" repeats the first week's pattern
        across the remaining weeks of the period.
FR-097  Helper: import from Google Calendar. Uses the free/busy lookup, which
        returns only busy time ranges and never event titles or details. A shift
        block is marked available only if the entire block is free. Events the
        user has marked "free" in Google do not block anything. The employee
        chooses which of their calendars count, reviews the result, and sends it
        themselves. Import is one-time per press, never a continuous sync.
FR-098  Submitting sends the availability to the admin. An employee may edit and
        resend as many times as they like until the schedule is generated.
FR-099  Anyone who has not submitted has the status "not sent". They are never
        treated as available. The admin can still place them on a shift by hand
        (FR-121).
FR-100  Availability locks the moment the admin starts building the schedule,
        whether by clicking Generate or by starting a manual build. Both paths
        behave identically.
FR-101  After the lock, an admin can reopen availability for one specific person
        without reopening it for everyone. That person is notified.
FR-102  Because availability locks, there is no "my availability changed after
        publishing" flow. After the lock, the only way out of a shift is to give
        it away (6.7). This is a deliberate simplification of the original idea
        and removes the auto-removal race it created.
FR-103  An employee may add one optional comment about their overall
        availability for the period: plain text, up to 500 characters,
        enforced on the server and not only in the text box. It is sent with
        the availability, locks with it (FR-100) and reopens with it (FR-101).
        Admins see it beside that person in the team availability view and, in
        short form, in the "add someone" list of the shift panel (FR-141).
        Coworkers never see it. People may explain their availability with
        health or family reasons, so it is treated as personal information
        (12.4, class B) and follows raw availability retention (section 13).
        It is rendered as text, never as HTML.

6.5 SCHEDULE GENERATION
FR-120  The admin clicks Generate and receives a complete draft schedule.
FR-121  The admin can build or edit entirely by hand. The editor allows placing
        someone who is unavailable or who never submitted; those assignments are
        shown in red and reported as issues. The generator never does this.
FR-122  Generation is seeded and randomized. Clicking Generate again produces a
        different valid schedule. The seed is stored so any result can be
        reproduced exactly.
FR-123  The admin can lock individual assignments. Locked assignments survive
        regeneration; everything else is rebuilt around them.
FR-124  "Reuse a past schedule" copies assignments from a previous period into
        the current one, then re-checks every assignment against the current
        availability and flags the conflicts.
FR-125  If a shift cannot be filled, the generator leaves the slot empty and
        reports it. It never fills a slot by breaking a hard rule.

6.6 MANUAL EDITING AND THE ISSUES DASHBOARD
FR-140  The schedule view shows the week as a grid of dated shift cards, each
        showing its time, its fill count (e.g. 2/2) and the people on it.
FR-141  Tapping a shift opens a panel with: the current roster, and a list of
        everyone who could be added. Every name carries a colour for their
        availability status (available / not available / no answer), their
        level, and a live hours counter for the week that shift falls in, shown
        as assigned/desired, e.g. 3/13.
FR-142  From that panel the admin can add someone, remove someone, or lock an
        assignment.
FR-143  The issues dashboard lists everything the evaluator found. Issue types:
          - shift below its headcount
          - shift missing a required level
          - person assigned who is not available
          - person assigned who never submitted availability
          - person under their desired hours
          - person over their desired hours
          - person working more consecutive days than allowed
          - person working both shifts in one day
        Employees see the subset that concerns them, plus open shifts.
FR-144  Each issue type has an admin setting controlling what happens at publish
        time: block publishing, ask for confirmation, or just display.
        Defaults: missing required level = block; below headcount = confirm;
        unavailable or unsubmitted person assigned = confirm; everything else
        = display.

6.7 PUBLISHING
FR-160  Publishing makes the draft visible to staff as version 1 and notifies
        everyone.
FR-161  After publishing, admin edits happen in a draft. An Update button
        appears only when the draft differs from what is live.
FR-162  On update, the app compares the draft against the live schedule as it
        stands at that moment, since trades and claims may have changed it in
        between. If a trade or claim touched the same shift, the admin is shown
        a conflict rather than silently overwriting it.
FR-163  Update bumps the version and notifies only the people whose own shifts
        changed, with a summary of what changed ("Thursday moved to 16:00").
        Everyone else is not notified.
FR-164  Every published version is retained as history within the retention
        window (section 13).
FR-165  A schedule may be published with empty slots. Those become open shifts
        that employees can claim.

6.8 GIVE AWAY, CLAIM, TRADE
FR-180  An employee can offer a published shift they hold. The shift is
        broadcast to the rest of the team as available to take.
FR-181  The person giving the shift away remains responsible for it until
        somebody else actually takes it. Offering does not remove them.
FR-182  There is no cutoff. A shift may be offered at any time before it starts.
FR-183  If an offered shift is still unclaimed X hours before it starts, the
        admins are notified. X is a workplace setting.
FR-184  An employee can claim an open slot or an offered shift.
FR-185  Claims are not restricted by desired hours, by level, or by whether the
        person marked themselves available. The app warns but does not block.
        The one hard block: a person cannot claim a shift that overlaps another
        shift they already hold, in this workplace or any other.
FR-186  A warning is shown when the claim would leave the shift without its
        required level.
FR-187  When approval for claims is switched on and several people request the
        same shift, the admin sees all requesters and picks one. Approving one
        automatically declines the others for that shift. When approval is off,
        the first claim wins.
FR-188  Trades are strictly one-for-one: one of my published shifts for one of
        yours.
FR-189  Trade lifecycle: requested -> the coworker accepts or declines -> if
        approval is on, the admin approves or declines -> applied.
FR-190  The requester can cancel a trade at any time until it is approved.
FR-191  A pending trade expires after 14 days, or when either shift starts,
        whichever comes first. It is also cancelled automatically if the admin
        edits either of the two shifts.
FR-192  At the moment of approval the app re-validates everything: both people
        still hold those shifts, required levels are still covered, and nobody
        ends up double-booked. A trade that no longer makes sense is rejected
        with an explanation, never applied blindly.
FR-193  Approval requirements are two independent settings: one for claims, one
        for trades.

6.9 NOTIFICATIONS
FR-210  Three channels: in-app, push, and email.
FR-211  Email is used for account essentials only: email verification and
        password reset. It is additionally used as a fallback for actionable
        items (a new or changed schedule, a trade waiting on you) for people who
        have not enabled push. Deliberately quiet by default.
FR-212  Push notifications are delivered through the installed PWA. See NFR-012.
FR-213  Notifications generated close together are bundled. Five edits in ten
        minutes produce one notification, not five.
FR-214  Philippe receives one email per month containing the final schedule for
        the month as PDF and CSV, so the business keeps its own records without
        the app storing them indefinitely.
FR-215  Notification events: availability requested; availability reminder;
        availability reopened for you; schedule published; your schedule changed;
        someone is giving away a shift; someone claims a shift (to admins);
        your claim was approved or declined; a trade request (to the coworker);
        a trade awaiting approval (to admins); an offered shift is still
        unclaimed (to admins); a member left or was removed (to admins);
        a new announcement (to all members, bundled per FR-213);
        (backlog) a new payslip is available (to that employee only).
FR-216  Notification and email text never carries sensitive content: no
        availability comments, no announcement photos, and (backlog) never pay
        amounts or payslip files. Push notifications appear on lock screens.
        Emails contain a link that requires signing in, never an attachment.
        The one exception is the owner's monthly schedule email (FR-214),
        which goes only to the owner and attaches the schedule as PDF and CSV.

6.10 ANNOUNCEMENTS (THE NEWS TAB)
FR-230  The landing page shows fixed cards: next shift, hours this week against
        target, open shifts, pending requests, and a preview of the latest
        announcement that opens the News tab.
FR-231  Announcements have their own tab, "News", for every member. Only
        holders of PostAnnouncements (owner and admins) can post. This is where
        Philippe posts recipes.
FR-232  A post is text, photos, or both. Up to 6 photos per post. Accepted:
        JPEG, PNG, WebP, and HEIC (converted on the server). Maximum 10 MB per
        photo before processing. Type is checked by file content, never by the
        file name or the browser's claim (AD-034).
FR-233  Every photo is re-encoded on the server before it is stored. This
        strips all metadata, including the GPS location phones embed in photos,
        and resizes to at most 2048 px on the long edge. Screenshots stay PNG so
        recipe text stays sharp; photos become JPEG. The browser also shrinks
        photos before upload to save mobile data, but the server never trusts
        that step.
FR-234  Members can react with a single thumbs-up. There are no replies or
        comments; announcements are one-way.
FR-235  Admins can edit and delete posts. Deleting a post deletes its photos
        from storage in the same operation.
FR-236  Photos open full screen inside the app. There is no download button.
        This is a deterrent, not a protection; see 12.6.
FR-237  Photos are private files scoped to the workplace (AD-017). They are
        only ever delivered through short-lived links issued to active members.
        When someone leaves or is removed, they lose access immediately.
FR-238  Posts and their photos are kept until an admin deletes them. Recipes
        are reference material, not personal information.

7. BUSINESS RULES: THE GENERATOR AND THE EVALUATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 One rulebook (AD-002)
A single Evaluator component grades any schedule and returns a score plus the
list of issues. The generator optimises that score. The dashboard displays that
same issue list. They cannot disagree, because they are the same code. Never
implement a second, parallel set of checks anywhere.

7.2 Generator interface (AD-003)
The generator is a pure function with no database, HTTP or time dependencies:

  input:  shifts, members (level, desired hours, max hours), availability,
          locked assignments, workplace rules, random seed
  output: assignments, issues

This makes it fast, deterministic for a given seed, and trivially testable.

7.3 Hard rules — the generator never breaks these
BR-001  Only people who marked themselves available for that exact shift.
BR-002  Nobody is assigned to two shifts that overlap in time, in this workplace
        or in another one.
BR-003  Never more people on a shift than its headcount.
BR-004  Locked assignments are preserved exactly.
BR-005  Nobody exceeds the workplace's maximum consecutive working days.
        This is a hard rule in every phase of the algorithm, including local
        search. (Learned the hard way in the prototype: when this was only a
        weighted penalty, a scarcity of level 3s made the optimiser put the
        owner on 28 straight shifts because covering a shift scored higher than
        the rest-day penalty. An absurd schedule is worse than a reported gap.)

7.4 Soft goals — optimised in this priority order
BR-010  Fill every slot.                                  (weight: highest)
BR-011  Satisfy each shift's level requirement.
BR-012  Get each person as close as possible to their desired weekly hours.
BR-013  Spread opening shifts fairly, if enabled.
BR-014  Spread closing shifts fairly, if enabled.
BR-015  Avoid giving one person both shifts in the same day. Permitted only as
        a last resort when no other assignment can fill the slot. An admin may
        still create a double by hand; it is flagged, not blocked.

7.5 Fairness definition
BR-020  Opening and closing fairness are two independent on/off settings.
BR-021  Fairness is proportional, not equal: someone working 30 hours should
        take more closings than someone working 10.
BR-022  Fairness is measured against what each person is actually available for.
        Expected openings = that person's total shifts multiplied by the share
        of openings within their own availability. An employee who is only ever
        free in the evenings is not penalised for never opening.
BR-023  There is no weekend fairness rule. With only two shifts per day, the
        opening and closing settings already balance days against evenings.

7.6 Other rules
BR-030  A shift is either fully covered by one person or not assigned to them.
        Splitting a shift between two people across its duration is not
        supported in v1.
BR-031  Hours count the full shift length. No unpaid break is deducted.
        Opening = 6 h, closing = 7 h.
BR-032  Desired hours is a single weekly target per person per workplace, with
        an optional admin-set maximum. There is no minimum.
BR-033  Under/over hours is reported when the total deviates from the target by
        more than 2 hours over the period.
BR-034  Opening and closing are identified automatically as the first and last
        shift block of each day. An admin can override the classification.
BR-035  Availability comments (FR-103) inform the admin's judgment only. The
        generator never reads them.

7.7 Algorithm (AD-004)
Randomized greedy construction followed by local search:
  1. Order shifts by scarcity (fewest available candidates first), with a small
     random jitter from the seed.
  2. For each shift, satisfy the level requirement first, then fill remaining
     slots. Candidates are scored on distance from their desired hours, with a
     random tiebreak among the top few. Hard rules filter the candidate pool.
  3. Local search: repeatedly try substituting one person for another and keep
     any change that improves the evaluator score. Hard rules are re-checked
     before any substitution is applied.

Scale: Presotea is 14 shifts and 28 slots per week over at most 4 weeks, with
15 people. The prototype runs a 2-week generation in roughly 150-200 ms in a
browser. The backend will be faster. If schedule quality ever disappoints,
Google OR-Tools CP-SAT can be swapped in behind the same interface without
changing anything else.

7.8 Testing the generator (AD-005)
  - Property tests: thousands of randomised inputs, asserting that no hard rule
    is ever broken, that no slot is overfilled and that locks always survive.
  - Golden tests: run against a real month of Presotea's Google Form responses
    and compare against the schedule Philippe built by hand.
  - Performance test: a 4-week period must generate in under 2 seconds.
Build and test the generator as a standalone library BEFORE building any UI
around it. If it does not produce schedules Philippe likes, that must surface
in week one, not after the screens are done.

8.2 Model decisions
AD-010  Template and dated shifts are separate. Starting a period copies the
        template into Shift rows. Editing the template later cannot corrupt a
        period already in progress. Availability points at Shift rows, which is
        what makes the tap-a-block UI trivial to store.
AD-011  Assignment and Shift both carry a tstzrange column holding the shift's
        start and end instants. The application computes it from the local
        date, local times and the workplace time zone; Postgres cannot generate
        it because time zone conversion is not an immutable function. A
        Postgres exclusion constraint on (user_id, time_range) over current
        live assignments (btree_gist plus a GiST index) makes double-booking
        impossible at the database level even if two requests arrive at the
        same instant, including across workplaces (BR-002). Drafts are checked
        by the evaluator instead. Ranges are half-open, so a shift ending at
        16:00 and one starting at 16:00 do not overlap.
AD-012  The published schedule is the source of truth for employees. The draft
        is admin-only. Publishing writes a ScheduleVersion and stamps the
        assignments belonging to it.
AD-013  Rows that several people can act on concurrently (Shift, Claim, Trade,
        GiveAway) carry a version column for optimistic concurrency. Two people
        tapping Claim at the same moment: one succeeds, the other is told the
        shift was just taken.
AD-014  Retired with chat on 2026-09-21. Number kept so references stay stable.
AD-015  Every module owns its tables in its own Postgres schema. Cross-module
        foreign keys are avoided in favour of module-level interfaces, so the
        boundaries stay real.
AD-016  AuditEvent records every schedule change with who, what and when. This
        is what answers "nobody told me I was working Saturday". It also records
        file uploads and deletions, permission grants, and (backlog) every read
        of a payslip. The same table serves both; the action name tells them
        apart.
AD-017  Every uploaded file goes through one StoredFile record and one storage
        service. Its scope decides who may read it: "workplace" means any active
        member of that workplace (announcement photos); "member" means that one
        member plus holders of a named permission (payslips). There is exactly
        one code path that issues download links, and it checks the scope every
        time. Adding payslips means adding a kind, not a second file system.

9. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 Shape of the system
  React PWA  ->  ASP.NET Core app (serves both the API and the built React app)
                     |
                     +-- PostgreSQL (Supabase, Montreal)
                     +-- Object storage (Supabase, private: announcement
                     |   photos now, payslips later)
                     +-- Email provider (transactional)
                     +-- Google APIs (sign-in, Calendar free/busy)
                     +-- Web Push (Apple / Google push services)
  Scheduled job  ->  calls one "tick" endpoint every 5 minutes

9.2 Backend: a modular monolith (AD-020)
One ASP.NET Core application containing strictly separated modules:
  Identity       accounts, login, tokens, email verification
  Workplaces     memberships, levels, settings, join codes, template
  Scheduling     periods, availability, drafts, generation, publishing
  Marketplace    give-aways, claims, trades, approvals
  Announcements  posts, photos, reactions
  Files          private storage, validation by content, image re-encoding,
                 download links, file access audit
  Payslips       (backlog) staged uploads, matching, publishing, recall
  Notifications  in-app, push, email dispatch, bundling
  Jobs           job queue and the tick command (called "Scheduler" in v1.0;
                 renamed so it cannot be confused with Scheduling)

Rules:
  - Each module owns its own database schema and exposes a small public
    interface. Modules never query another module's tables.
  - An automated architecture test (NetArchTest or ArchUnitNET) fails the build
    if a module references another module's internals.
  - The generator is a separate class library with no dependency on the web
    stack or the database.

Why not microservices: at 15 users they would add network failures, distributed
transactions and deployment complexity for no benefit. The module boundaries
mean any piece could be extracted later if it ever needs to be. This is the
choice a senior developer makes at this scale, and the discipline is enforced
by a test rather than by good intentions.

9.3 Inside a module
  - Organised by feature. One use case ("publish schedule") is one folder
    holding its endpoint, handler, validation and tests.
  - Business rules live in domain classes, not in endpoints.
  - Plain handler classes; no MediatR and no AutoMapper. Both moved to
    commercial licensing in 2025 and neither earns its dependency here.
  - EF Core with Npgsql for data access. Raw SQL where a query genuinely needs
    it, which is fine and expected.

9.4 API
AD-021  REST with an OpenAPI description generated from the code.
AD-022  The TypeScript client used by React is generated from that description
        in CI. A renamed field breaks the frontend build instead of breaking
        production silently.
AD-023  Errors are returned as machine-readable codes plus a problem-details
        body. The client renders the translated message. The server never sends
        user-facing English.

9.5 Authentication and authorisation
AD-024  ASP.NET Core Identity with cookie authentication. Cookies work cleanly
        because the API and the web app share one origin. Includes password
        hashing, reset tokens, email confirmation and lockout out of the box.
AD-025  Anti-forgery protection on all state-changing requests.
AD-026  Every request resolves the caller's membership in the target workplace
        before anything else. Authorisation is membership-based, never
        role-on-account.
AD-027  Integration tests prove that an employee cannot reach admin endpoints,
        that no member can read another workplace's data, that no employee can
        read another member's availability comment, and that no file link is
        issued to a non-member, a removed member, or (backlog) anyone but the
        employee and ManagePayslips holders for a payslip.
AD-042  Authorisation is by named permission (section 5.5), never by comparing
        role names.
AD-043  Two-factor authentication with an authenticator app (time-based codes),
        using ASP.NET Core Identity's built-in support, plus one-time recovery
        codes. Built in M2 and optional for everyone. Mandatory for anyone
        holding ManagePayslips before payslips launch.
AD-044  Step-up authentication: sensitive actions require the password to have
        been confirmed within the last 10 minutes. Applies to changing email,
        transferring ownership, regenerating the join code, granting
        permissions, turning off two-factor, and (backlog) opening payslips.
AD-045  Session cookies are HttpOnly, Secure and SameSite=Lax. Sessions are
        stored server-side so signing out, a password reset, or "sign out of all
        devices" revokes them immediately. ASP.NET Core Data Protection keys are
        persisted in Postgres; otherwise every scale-to-zero restart would
        invalidate every cookie, antiforgery token and emailed link.

9.6 Time
AD-028  NodaTime throughout. A shift is stored as a local date plus local times
        plus the workplace's time zone, and converted to an instant only when
        scheduling a notification. This is what keeps a 16:00 shift at 16:00
        across daylight saving transitions.

9.7 Background work
AD-029  Hosted background services and in-process timers are not reliable on a
        platform that scales to zero. Instead: a ScheduledJob table, and a
        Container Apps scheduled job that runs the application's own image with
        the "tick" command every 5 minutes. The command runs whatever is due,
        idempotently, and exits. It does not call the web app over HTTP: the
        app scales to zero only after 300 seconds without requests, so an HTTP
        tick every 5 minutes would keep it awake permanently and use up the
        free grant. In development, POST /internal/tick runs one tick.
        Jobs: send availability requests, send reminders, alert on unclaimed
        offered shifts, expire trades, delete data past retention, send the
        monthly schedule email, retry outbox messages.

9.8 Notifications
AD-030  Outbox pattern. The business change and its "send this" record are
        written in the same database transaction, then delivered separately
        with retries. A failing email provider can never lose a notification or
        roll back a schedule publish. Delivery is attempted right after the
        transaction commits; the tick only retries failures, so account emails
        arrive in seconds rather than at the next tick.

9.9 Real-time
AD-031  No persistent real-time connection in v1. With chat removed, nothing
        needs instant delivery. Screens refetch when the app returns to the
        foreground and on a modest interval where freshness matters (requests
        inbox, open shifts). Push notifications carry anything urgent. This
        removes WebSockets, their hosting cost while connections are open, and
        the old open question on real-time delivery.
AD-032  Retired with chat. Number kept so references stay stable.
AD-033  If live updates are ever needed, SignalR can be added later as a
        "this changed" signal on top of the same REST API, without changing it.

9.10 File uploads and downloads
AD-034  Uploads go through the API, not straight from the browser to storage.
        The API checks the uploader's permission, identifies the type from the
        file's first bytes (never the name or the browser's claim), enforces the
        size limit, re-encodes images (FR-233), and only then writes to private
        storage. Nothing unvalidated ever lands in storage. With chat gone, the
        only uploads are admin photos up to 10 MB and, later, PDFs up to 5 MB,
        so passing them through the API is cheap. SVG is refused outright
        because it can carry scripts.
AD-047  Downloads use links valid for 5 minutes, issued only after the scope
        check (AD-017), and served with "no-store" caching and a strict
        content type. Link issuing is rate limited per user, so nobody can
        quietly pull every file. The storage bucket is private; nothing is ever
        publicly readable.

9.11 Frontend
AD-035  Vite + React + TypeScript. Not Next.js: the C# backend already does the
        server-side work, and a second server-side framework would only add a
        deployment target and confusion about where logic lives.
AD-036  TanStack Query for all server state. React state for UI state only.
AD-037  react-hook-form with zod for forms and validation. Validation rules are
        shared with the generated API types where possible.
AD-038  Tailwind for styling with Radix primitives underneath anything
        interactive (dialogs, menus, switches), which gives keyboard and screen
        reader behaviour for free.
AD-039  react-i18next for translations, with fr and en resource files.
AD-040  vite-plugin-pwa for the installable app, push notifications and an
        offline read-only copy of your own shifts. The offline cache is an
        allowlist: the app shell and the user's own published shifts, nothing
        else. It never stores files from storage (announcement photos,
        payslips) or any response marked no-store. This matters on shared and
        lost phones.
AD-041  Frontend folders mirror the backend modules so that a feature lives in
        one obvious place on both sides.
AD-048  Design tokens are defined once, as Tailwind theme variables in
        src/web/src/styles/index.css: colours, type, radii, shadows and the one
        motion curve (section 19). Components use token names (bg-primary,
        rounded-card, shadow-press) and never a literal colour, radius or
        shadow. Changing the palette or the typeface is then one edit, and a
        screen that drifts from the design is visible in review.

9.12 Browser security headers
AD-046  Every response carries: a strict Content-Security-Policy (no inline
        scripts, images only from the app and the storage domain, no framing by
        other sites), Strict-Transport-Security, X-Content-Type-Options:
        nosniff, Referrer-Policy: no-referrer (so download links never leak to
        other sites), and a Permissions-Policy that turns off camera,
        microphone and location. User text (announcements, availability
        comments) is always rendered as text, never as HTML.

10.2 Why PostgreSQL (AD-050)
The data is deeply relational: users, memberships, workplaces, shifts,
assignments, claims and trades all reference each other. Claims and trades need
real transactions so two people cannot take the same shift. Postgres exclusion
constraints prevent double-booking at the storage layer, which no amount of
application code can guarantee as reliably. EF Core's Postgres support is
mature, and Supabase offers a free tier with a Montreal region.

10.3 Why Azure Container Apps (AD-051)
Decided in preference to Google Cloud Run, which is technically equivalent for
this workload. The deciding factor is career value: Azure is the common cloud
in companies hiring .NET developers, and this project doubles as internship
evidence. Supporting facts: the free monthly grant (180,000 vCPU-seconds,
360,000 GiB-seconds, 2 million requests) far exceeds what 15 users generate;
nothing is billed while the app is asleep; scheduled jobs are built into the
platform; and Canada East is in Quebec City, which keeps the workload in the
province.
  Trade-offs accepted: more concepts to set up than Cloud Run; Azure Container
  Registry is not free (~$0.167/day at its cheapest), which is why images live
  on GitHub's registry instead; Azure's default logging configuration is a
  well-known source of surprise costs and must be kept minimal; Quebec City is
  ~250 km from the Montreal database, adding a few milliseconds per query.

10.4 Rejected hosting options and why
  Vercel            The free Hobby plan is restricted to non-commercial personal
                    use, and a tool built for a business is at best a grey area.
                    Free-plan scheduled functions run at most once a day within
                    an imprecise window, which cannot support "warn 24 hours
                    before an unclaimed shift". WebSocket support only reached
                    public beta in June 2026, pins connections to one instance,
                    and caps them at 5 minutes by default. (The WebSocket point
                    no longer matters after chat was removed; the other reasons
                    stand.) Vercel also has no first-class .NET support; it
                    would require containerising anyway.
  Google Cloud Run  A good option and the runner-up. Simpler setup and Montreal
                    region. Rejected only on career value.
  Azure App Service Free F1 tier allows 60 CPU-minutes per day, forbids custom
                    domains, and unloads the app after ~20 minutes idle. It is
                    a testing tier.
  Render            Easiest setup and needs no credit card, but the free service
                    sleeps after 15 minutes and takes about a minute to wake,
                    and there is no Canadian region. Philippe would wait every
                    morning, and the data would leave Quebec.
  Oracle Cloud      A genuinely always-free always-on VM in Montreal, but it
                    makes the developer the system administrator (patching,
                    TLS, restarts, backups), and Oracle reclaims free instances
                    that stay under 20% CPU for a week, which this app would.
  AWS Lambda        Would require reshaping the app into many small functions
                    plus separate services for real-time messaging. More moving
                    parts, no benefit at this scale.
  Azure for Students Needs no credit card, but is limited to learning and
                    development use and stops when the credit or the year runs
                    out. Use it for experiments; never for Philippe's live data.

10.5 Other rejected choices
  Microservices     See 9.2.
  Next.js           See AD-035.
  MediatR/AutoMapper Commercial licensing since 2025; unnecessary at this size.
  In-memory test DB Hides real Postgres behaviour, especially constraints and
                    concurrency. Tests run against real Postgres in a container.
  Chat              Removed from the product on 2026-09-21 (3.4).
  WebSockets/SignalR in v1   Nothing needs them without chat (AD-031).
  Direct browser-to-storage uploads   Were chosen for 50 MB chat videos. The
                    only uploads left are small admin files, and routing them
                    through the API is what lets the server validate and
                    re-encode everything (AD-034).
  Blocking screenshots of recipes   Not possible on the web, and no app can
                    stop a second phone photographing the screen (12.6).
  Continuous Google Calendar sync   Would produce a stream of change
                    notifications to the admin and would need broader, more
                    invasive calendar permissions. One-time free/busy import
                    instead (FR-097).

11.3 CI/CD (AD-061)

11.4 Domain (AD-062)
A custom domain under $30/year is required before the pilot with real staff,
not after. Reasons: installed PWAs and their push subscriptions are bound to
the origin, so changing the address later forces every person to reinstall and
re-enable notifications; transactional email providers either require a
verified domain to send to third parties or deliver poorly without one, which
would put password resets in spam; Google's OAuth verification requires one.
Check the renewal price, not just the first-year price.

11.5 Monitoring and backups
  - Structured logging with Serilog, kept deliberately small to avoid log costs.
  - A health endpoint plus an external uptime check that emails on failure.
  - A nightly database dump we control, stored in Montreal, independent of the
    provider's own backups. The dataset is small enough that this costs cents.
    Backups are encrypted with a key stored separately from them, because they
    contain availability comments now and would contain payslip records
    later. Storage files (recipe photos) are backed up too, encrypted the same
    way.
  - Two-factor authentication is turned on for every infrastructure account
    (GitHub, Azure, Supabase, the domain registrar, the email provider) before
    any real data exists. These accounts can reach everything.
  - A budget alert set at $1 on the cloud account so any charge is noticed
    immediately.

12. PRIVACY, SECURITY AND LEGAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.1 Quebec Law 25
The app holds personal information about Quebec employees, so Law 25 applies.
The developer is not a lawyer and this section is not legal advice; it records
the practical decisions taken.
  - Personal information is stored in Quebec: database and file storage in
    Montreal, application in Quebec City. Law 25 requires an assessment before
    personal information is communicated outside Quebec; keeping it in-province
    avoids that question for the main data store.
  - The transactional email provider will likely process data outside Quebec.
    This is the one known exception and should be raised with Philippe.
  - A privacy policy is required and must state what is collected, why, who can
    see it, and the rules for keeping and destroying it (section 13).
  - The business is responsible for the personal information it holds. Since
    the developer personally owns the infrastructure accounts, the arrangement
    between Hoang and Philippe should be written down, including who is
    responsible for the data and how it is protected. It does not need to be a
    long document.

12.2 Data minimisation
  - No phone numbers are collected.
  - Google Calendar integration reads free/busy only, never event details.
  - Employees see only names, levels, work email and the schedule.
  - Availability comments are visible only to their author and admins.
  - Photos have their location metadata removed before storage (FR-233).
  - The platform admin account is not a workplace member and cannot read
    availability comments or, later, payslips through the app.

12.3 Security practices
  - Passwords: minimum 8 characters, breached-password check, hashing by
    ASP.NET Core Identity, temporary lockout after repeated failures.
  - Single-use, short-lived tokens for password reset; reset invalidates other
    sessions.
  - Rate limiting on login, password reset and join code attempts.
  - Anti-forgery protection on state-changing requests.
  - Authorisation checked per membership on every request, with tests proving
    cross-workplace and cross-user access is impossible.
  - Files served through 5-minute links from private buckets, never public
    (AD-047). Uploads validated by content and images re-encoded (AD-034).
  - Optional two-factor authentication for everyone; step-up confirmation for
    sensitive actions (AD-043, AD-044).
  - Security headers and a strict Content-Security-Policy (AD-046).
  - Dependency scanning (Dependabot) and secret scanning enabled on the repo.

12.4 Classes of sensitive data
Every piece of data belongs to one class, and the class decides the controls.

  Class A  Highly sensitive personal information: payslips (backlog).
           Member-scoped files; ManagePayslips permission; two-factor required
           for uploaders; step-up to open; every read audited; staged
           publishing and recall; never cached offline; never in notification
           text; longer-kept access log.
  Class B  Personal information: names, emails, levels, availability,
           availability comments, schedules.
           Membership-based access; comments admin-only; retention per
           section 13.
  Class C  Business-confidential, not personal: recipe photos and
           announcements.
           Workplace-scoped private files; metadata stripped; 5-minute links;
           access ends the moment someone leaves; not cached offline; no
           download button.

Law 25 covers classes A and B. Class C is protected because it matters to the
business, not because the law requires it.

12.5 Access by the developer
Hoang owns the infrastructure accounts, so he can technically read anything in
the database or storage, including, later, payslips. The app cannot remove that
completely, so it is managed instead:
  - two-factor on every infrastructure account (11.5);
  - no routine access to production data; any access is for a stated support
    reason;
  - the written arrangement with Philippe (12.1) says who can access what and
    why;
  - for payslips, an optional further step: encrypt each payslip file with a
    key held in Azure Key Vault, so that reaching the storage bucket alone is
    not enough to read one. This reduces exposure; it does not eliminate it,
    since the same person administers Key Vault.

12.6 What the app cannot prevent
Anyone allowed to see a recipe photo can screenshot it, or photograph the
screen with another phone. No website can block that reliably, and no app can
stop a second device. The app's job is to limit who can see recipes, end
access the moment someone leaves, and record who posted what. Keeping recipes
confidential beyond that is an employment matter for Philippe, for example a
confidentiality clause (Q-12).

12.7 Payslip readiness: what v1 must already contain
These are built as part of v1 even though payslips are not, because each one

12.8 Confidentiality incidents
Law 25 requires the business to keep a register of confidentiality incidents
and, when an incident presents a risk of serious harm, to notify the

meaning Philippe, is responsible; the developer supports him. I am not a
lawyer; this records the practical plan. Before payslips launch, write a
one-page runbook: how to recall a file and invalidate its links, how to use
the audit log to see who opened it, who tells whom, and where the register
lives. A payslip published to the wrong person is an incident.

13. DATA RETENTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Storage is not the constraint; a shift assignment is a tiny row. Retention here
is about privacy, relevance, and Law 25's requirement that the business state
its retention and destruction rules.

  Announcements and their photos  until an admin deletes them
  Notifications                   60 days
  Raw availability submissions    60 days after the period ends
  Availability comments           60 days after the period ends
  Published schedules             2 months
  Audit events                    2 months
  Generation runs (seed, score)   2 months
  Payslips (backlog)              to be decided before build (Q-11)
  Payslip access log (backlog)    12 months, so incidents can be investigated
  Accounts                        until deleted by the user

Supporting rules:
  - Deletion runs nightly as a scheduled job, never manually.
  - Admins can export any schedule to CSV and PDF at any time.
  - Philippe automatically receives each month's final schedule by email as
    PDF and CSV (FR-214), so the business keeps records beyond the app's
    retention window without the app holding them.
  - Deleted accounts: profile and availability comments removed; past
    schedules show "Former employee"; their announcements show
    "Former member".

in memory and no backend. It resets on reload.

What it implements faithfully and can be used as a reference for behaviour:
  - The generator and evaluator sharing one rulebook (AD-002)
  - Hard rules including max consecutive days (BR-001..005)
  - Soft goal ordering and availability-proportional fairness (BR-010..015,
    BR-020..022)
  - Seeded regeneration and locked assignments (FR-122, FR-123)
  - Availability locking on generate, and per-person reopen (FR-100, FR-101)
  - Publishing, update with version bump, notifying only affected people
  - Give away, claim with admin picking among competitors, one-for-one trades
  - Admin-assigned levels affecting generation immediately
  - Publish checks (block / confirm / show) per issue type
  - French and English throughout, light and dark mode
  - The News tab: admins post text and up to 6 photos; each photo is redrawn
    on a canvas before posting, which strips its metadata as FR-233 requires;
    type and size are checked; members react; admins delete
  - The optional availability comment: saved with the submission, capped at
    500 characters in the reducer itself, locked with availability, shown to
    the admin in the team list and in the shift panel, hidden from coworkers

  - Sign in, sign up with full validation, email verification, forgot and
    reset password (FR-001..007)
  - The workplace list as the landing screen after login, join by code, and the
    three-step create-workplace wizard restricted to the platform admin
    (FR-030..034)
  - Template editing: times snapped to 15 minutes, per-block days, headcount,
    required level, and a refusal to create a shift crossing midnight
    (FR-060..067)

What it deliberately fakes:
  - Passwords are not checked; any 8+ characters signs you in as the account
    matching the email. Three prototype accounts are offered as fill-in chips.
  - Verification and reset emails are replaced by an "open the link" button
  - Google Calendar import is simulated locally
  - Notifications are in-app only
  - Trade expiry is displayed but not enforced by a timer
  - Photos are kept in memory as data URLs. There is no private storage, no
    download link, and no server-side re-encoding; those exist only in the
    real backend (AD-017, AD-034, AD-047).
  - Only JPEG, PNG and WebP are accepted, because browsers cannot reliably
    decode HEIC. The real backend converts HEIC on the server (FR-232).
  - None of the payslip readiness items (12.7) can be shown in a single-file
    prototype. They are backend work.
  - Only Presotea holds live data. A workplace created through the wizard shows
    its join code and the template you built, but has no members, so it cannot
    collect availability or generate. Adding a second live workplace needs the
    per-workplace data separation the real backend has by design.
  - The Presotea template keeps exactly two blocks (opening and closing) so the
    shift labels stay meaningful. The wizard allows any number of blocks.
  - The "view as" dropdown stays in the top bar as a prototype tool for jumping
    between people without signing out. It does not exist in the real product.

Seed data mirrors the shop: 15 people, two shifts a day, two per shift, level 3
required on every shift, five level 3s including Philippe, two people who have
not submitted availability. Editing the PEOPLE array at the top of the file
changes the scenario.

19. VISUAL DESIGN (THE LOOK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19.1 Where the design lives
The design prototype, docs/prototype/quart-design-prototype.html, is the
visual reference. Open it in a browser: it is a phone-sized, clickable version
of Home, Schedule, Availability, Requests, News, the shift sheet and Settings,
in both the employee and the owner view. docs/design/README.md is the written
version with every token; this section is the short form, so the spec stays
readable on its own. The tokens are real code in src/web/src/styles/index.css
(AD-048); no screen writes a colour, radius or shadow inline (NFR-017).

19.2 The feel
Friendly and physical, not corporate. Rounded type, a pale periwinkle
background, white cards that float softly, and buttons that sit on a hard 3 px
edge so they look pressable. Big headings, generous spacing, everything
comfortably reachable with a thumb. It should look like a tool made for a
bubble tea shop, not a payroll system. Colour always carries meaning, and
never carries it alone (NFR-008).

19.3 Colour
  bg            #E9EEFD  the app background
  surface       #FFFFFF  cards
  surface-2     #FBFBFD  sheets and secondary panels
  surface-3     #EEF0F7  rows and wells inside a card
  line          #DCE3FA  hairlines, used sparingly
  ink           #1B1B22  all primary text, and solid dark buttons
  muted         #5E6380  subtitles, labels, secondary detail
  link          #3F5FC4  links and the focus ring
  primary       #7F9FF2  hero cards, the selected pill, the logo blob
  accent        #F48DA0  badges and counts only, never a large area
  on-primary    #1B1B22  text drawn on primary or accent, in both themes
  success       #11643A on #DDF3E4   sent, covered, approved
  danger        #8E1A33 on #FDE1E6   missing level 3, blocked, not available
  warning       #6B4A00 on #FFF0C9   reopened, confirm before publishing
  neutral       #4A4F66 on #ECEDF3   no answer yet
  opening       #CDD35B  the opening shift's flavour
  closing       #ADA8DA  the closing shift's flavour

Every text-on-background pair above meets WCAG 2.2 AA at 4.5:1 (NFR-009). The
single exception is white on primary, which appears only as the wordmark
inside the logo blob: a logotype, which WCAG excludes, and never text.

Dark mode (NFR-011) keeps every role and hue and darkens the surfaces. It is
derived, not designed, because the prototype is light only (19.7). Primary and
accent keep their light values there, which is why text on them uses
on-primary and never ink: ink flips to near-white in dark mode and would drop
to 2.2:1 on that blue.

19.4 Type
Quicksand carries the whole interface, mostly at 600 and 700; 400 is never
used for interface text. Titan One appears in exactly two places: the Quart
wordmark and the join code. Both are self-hosted through Fontsource, so no
visitor's IP address reaches a font CDN (10.1, 12.2).
  Screen title    700, 32 px, line-height 1.1, tracking -0.01em
  Card title      700, 17-24 px
  Body            600, 14-15 px, line-height 1.4-1.5
  Group label     700, 12 px, uppercase, tracking 0.06-0.08em
  Helper text     600, 12-15 px in muted

19.5 Shape, elevation and motion
Pills (99 px) for everything tappable: buttons, chips, filters, tabs, badges.
Rounded rectangles for anything holding content: 28 px cards, 32 px hero
cards, 20 px fields, 18 px chips, 36 px on a sheet's top corners. Circles for
avatars, the settings button and the plus/minus steppers. The logo is a
deliberately uneven blob, tilted -8 degrees, so it reads as drawn.

Two shadows do the work of borders: a soft ambient shadow under cards, and a
hard 3 px offset under anything tappable. A third, heavier shadow lifts the
floating tab bar and open sheets.

Sheets slide up in 0.28 s, backdrops fade in 0.2 s, toasts pop in 0.25 s.
Nothing else animates, and everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion
(NFR-010).

19.6 Screen patterns
  - Top row: the logo blob on the left; on the right the settings circle for
    admins (NFR-016) and the person's initials in a white circle.
  - Header: a muted context line, then the 32 px title ("Hi Sophie!").
  - Home: one hero card in primary with a large translucent circle bleeding
    off its corner, holding whatever matters most right now (the next shift
    for an employee, the state of the next period for the owner), then smaller
    cards below.
  - Tab bar: a floating white pill above the bottom edge, at most five tabs,
    the selected one filled with primary, counts in an accent badge.
  - Detail opens in a bottom sheet, not a new page: the shift panel, publish
    confirmation and settings all use one sheet component.
  - Status chips pair a soft background with its strong colour and always
    carry a word or a symbol.
  - The mascot: a bubble tea cup drawn in CSS, with a flavour colour and a
    mood (happy, sleepy, surprised). Opening shifts are lime, closing shifts
    are lavender; the cup appears on the next-shift card, on empty states and
    on the publish sheet. It is decoration and is hidden from screen readers;
    it never carries information on its own.
  - Tap targets are at least 44 px, usually 48 px.
  - Tone of voice is part of the design: short, human, second person. Write
    the French first where you can; it is the shop's working language.

19.7 What the design prototype does not cover
  - Dark mode. The prototype is light only; the dark palette is derived and
    must be checked on a real phone before the pilot (Q-14).
  - Desktop and iPad. The same components in a wider column with a sidebar
    instead of the tab bar (NFR-003), not yet drawn.
  - Sign-up, sign-in, the workplace list and the template editor, which the
    behaviour prototype covers instead (section 18).
  - Error, loading and offline states beyond the empty states shown.
  - Longer French strings. French runs longer than English; every screen is
    checked in French at 390 px before it is called done (NFR-013).
